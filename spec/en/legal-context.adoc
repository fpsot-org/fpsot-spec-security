= Laws and deadlines of recipients
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

This page is informative.
It sets no rule, and it is not legal advice.
It sums up what some laws and government rules say on vulnerabilities.
Readers can then see how the rules of this specification fit with them.
A lawyer must confirm each point before {abbr-fpsot-first} relies on it.

== No law sets an embargo

None of the laws that {abbr-fpsot} has read requires a private head start, forbids one or sets its length.
The periods of 72 hours in these laws are deadlines to tell an authority of an incident.
They are not a minimum or a maximum time before public disclosure.
The head start of SEC-054 rests on the practice of other open-source projects, not on a law.

== The European Union Cyber Resilience Act

The link:https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/2847/oj/eng[Cyber Resilience Act] defines an open-source software steward.
A steward is a legal person, other than a manufacturer, that supports free software meant for commercial use.
Its support is systematic and lasting, and the recitals name foundations as examples.
The research of {abbr-fpsot} suggests that it is likely a steward for some components.
These are the components that others build into products sold in the European Union.
Its status depends on each product and on how it funds its work, so a lawyer must decide it.

From 11 December 2027, a steward must have a documented cybersecurity policy that covers vulnerability handling.
It must also report each actively exploited {term-vulnerability} to the extent that it takes part in the development.
Such a report goes through the single reporting platform of ENISA.
The act sets an early warning within 24 hours and a notification within 72 hours.
A final report follows within 14 days of a fix, and the act sets no fines for stewards.
This specification aims to give {abbr-fpsot} that policy, and SEC-055 already releases at once when attackers exploit a flaw.

== Deadlines of recipients in Canada

Federal departments have their own deadlines to fix a {term-vulnerability} once they learn of it.
The federal link:https://www.canada.ca/en/government/system/digital-government/online-security-privacy/patch-management-guidance.html[Patch Management Guidance] suggests deadlines by priority.
It suggests deploying an extreme patch within 48 hours of notification, and a high one within 2 weeks.
The link:https://www.canada.ca/en/government/system/digital-government/policies-standards/spin/direction-government-canada-cyber-security-readiness-frontier-artificial-intelligence-era.html[direction on cyber security readiness] of October 2026 asks departments to act on the alerts of vendors.
Each department must triage each high or critical alert within 10 business days, with a documented response.
A pre-notice with a tested fix helps a department meet these targets.

link:https://www.parl.ca/legisinfo/en/bill/45-1/c-8[Bill C-8] received Royal Assent in June 2026.
Its Part 2, the Critical Cyber Systems Protection Act, is not yet in force.
It would bind only designated operators of federal critical systems.
It sets no rule on how a vendor discloses a {term-vulnerability}.

== Deadlines of recipients in Québec

The Québec link:https://cdn-contenu.quebec.ca/cdn-contenu/adm/min/cybersecurite_numerique/Publications/DIR_securite_information_2021.pdf[Directive gouvernementale sur la sécurité de l’information] dates from 2021.
It asks each public body to report a security event with a risk of serious harm without delay.
It gives no number of hours, and it sets no deadline to deploy a fix.
The law of Québec on personal information applies only when personal information leaks.
A {term-vulnerability} on its own is not an incident under that law.
