= Reporting and handling vulnerabilities
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

These rules set how a person reports a {term-vulnerability} in an {abbr-fpsot-first} project, and how the project handles the report.
xref:disclosure.adoc[Pre-notice and disclosure] sets who learns of it, and when.

== Reports stay out of Working Groups

Each {abbr-fpsot} specification repository holds the link:https://github.com/CommunitySpecification/Community_Specification/blob/8a05e46d768ac0525af882b619c42f3380a9e869/GOVERNANCE.md[governance file of the Community Specification 1.0].
Its section 5 says that information shared in Working Group activity is not confidential.
That covers meetings, contributions and submissions, whatever their markings.
In a private repository, participants also need the approval of the Working Group to disclose it in public.
Even then, every participant can read a report, and no duty of confidentiality binds them.
The rules below therefore keep every report outside Working Group activity.

[[SEC-040]]
*SEC-040.* Each repository MUST offer a private channel to report a {term-vulnerability}, such as the reporting feature of its forge.
The channel MUST deliver each report only to the security contacts of SEC-002 and to the security team.
The foundation MUST also offer a second private channel for people without an account, such as an encrypted email address.
A report MUST NOT go through a Working Group, a public issue or discussion, a mailing list or a meeting.
A private channel lets the project fix the flaw before attackers learn of it.

[[SEC-041]]
*SEC-041.* A maintainer who finds a report in a public place MUST hide or remove it at once, where possible.
The maintainer MUST then ask the reporter to use the private channel, and MUST tell the security team.
The security team MUST treat that {term-vulnerability} as possibly public under SEC-055.
A report that anyone could read may already be in the hands of an attacker.

[[SEC-042]]
*SEC-042.* {abbr-fpsot} MUST have a security team that handles each report outside Working Group activity.
The team MUST have at least three members, and {abbr-fpsot} MUST publish their roles and how to reach them.
Each member MUST agree in writing to keep reports and fixes confidential until their public release.
Anyone else who can see private reports, such as an administrator or an organization owner, MUST sign the same pledge.
The team decides the severity, the dates of the pre-notice and of the public release, and any early release.
A small, known group with a duty of confidentiality is what makes private handling possible.

== Times

[[SEC-043]]
*SEC-043.* The security team MUST acknowledge each report within 3 business days of its receipt.
Within 10 business days, it MUST tell the reporter whether it accepts the report, and with what severity.
It SHOULD then tell the reporter of its progress at least every 14 days, until the public release.
A reporter who hears nothing may go public sooner, and fast answers keep the reporter working with the project.

== Severity

[[SEC-044]]
*SEC-044.* The security team MUST score each accepted {term-vulnerability} in software with link:https://www.first.org/cvss/v4.0/specification-document[CVSS v4.0].
It MUST record the full vector string, starting with `CVSS:4.0/`, and the severity rating that the vector gives.
It MUST publish the base score with the label `CVSS-B`, as the specification names it.
It MAY add the threat metrics when it knows of exploitation, with the label `CVSS-BT`.
It MUST NOT use an older version of {abbr-cvss-first} for a new {term-vulnerability}.
Adopters compare scores across products, and this only works with one version and the full vector.

== Identifiers and advisories

[[SEC-045]]
*SEC-045.* The security team MUST obtain a {abbr-cve-first} identifier for each {term-vulnerability} it fixes in software.
It uses a {abbr-cve} Numbering Authority, such as that of the forge or of a body that covers the product.
Each flaw that a team can fix on its own MUST get its own identifier.
The link:https://www.cve.org/ResourcesSupport/AllResources/CNARules[CNA Operational Rules] require this.
A {abbr-cve} identifier lets every scanner and every adopter track the same flaw under one name.

[[SEC-046]]
*SEC-046.* The security team MUST publish a security advisory for each fixed {term-vulnerability}, at its public release.
The advisory MUST follow the link:https://ossf.github.io/osv-schema/[OSV schema], and MUST appear in the advisory database of the forge, if any.
It MUST give the {abbr-cve} identifier, the affected and fixed versions, the {abbr-cvss} vector and a description.
It MUST also give any workaround, and the dates of the report, the pre-notice and the release.
It MUST credit the reporter, under the name the reporter chooses, unless the reporter asks for no credit.
Machines read the {abbr-osv-first} format, so scanners can warn each adopter who runs an affected version.

== After the fix

[[SEC-047]]
*SEC-047.* The project MUST add a test that fails on the flaw and passes on the fix, under SEC-083.
The team MUST look for the root cause and for the same flaw elsewhere in its code.
It SHOULD record what it changes in its process to prevent a repeat.
A fix of one instance leaves the others in place if nobody looks for the cause.

[[SEC-048]]
*SEC-048.* The security team MUST keep a private record of each report, with the date of each step.
The record MUST hold each decision with its reason.
It MUST keep the record for at least 5 years after the public release.
A record shows that each report got the same treatment, and it answers the questions of adopters and auditors.

== Flaws in outside components

[[SEC-049]]
*SEC-049.* When a {term-vulnerability} sits in an outside component, the security team MUST follow the embargo of its maintainers.
It MUST NOT disclose the flaw, or a fix that reveals it, before the date that they set.
It MAY send a pre-notice to the members of SEC-050 only for what these maintainers permit.
The team SHOULD report the flaw to these maintainers first, if they do not know of it.
Breaking the embargo of another project would harm its users, and those of {abbr-fpsot} too.
